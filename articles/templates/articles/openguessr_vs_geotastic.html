{% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr and Geotastic both give you the whole game for nothing in principle, and they spread in different directions. OpenGuessr adds separate ways to play: Country Guessr, Image Guessr, Meta Guessr, competitions and a free education site. Geotastic, the oldest game in this corner of the genre, adds variations on the classic round instead: blitz duels, highscore hunts, country streak rules, seasons, GeoBingo, and a Twitch mode where a streamer's chat submits the guesses. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} The other difference is funding. OpenGuessr locks nothing and runs one ad slot during play. Geotastic runs on donations, and some of its features stay locked until you donate or until other players cover the month's Google bill. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #heading_primary %}Overview{% /heading_primary %} {% #comparison_table col_a="OpenGuessr" col_b="Geotastic" %} {% comparison_row label="Price" a="Free, including every mode." b="Free to play, with custom lobbies and other features behind a donation or community funding." %} {% comparison_row label="Account" a="Optional, custom rooms included." b="Required for most of the game." %} {% comparison_row label="Singleplayer" a="Endless rounds, with an optional timer." b="Quick play, highscore hunts and challenges across many rule sets." %} {% comparison_row label="Other modes" a="Country Guessr, Image Guessr, Meta Guessr, competitions and the OpenGuessr Education quizzes." b="Flag rounds, aerial rounds, country streaks, GeoBingo, seasons and a Twitch mode." %} {% comparison_row label="Editors" a="Map and competition creation, with locations uploadable as JSON." b="Map editor and challenge editor, both unusually detailed." %} {% comparison_row label="Multiplayer" a="Custom rooms, free to host and open to players without an account." b="Custom local and online lobbies, from supporter level 1." %} {% comparison_row label="Ranked" a="Elo divisions, tokens for wins, a duel history and a choice of matchmaking pool." b="Matchmaking, blitz duels and seasonal ranked play." %} {% comparison_row label="Social" a="Friends with online status, invites from the multiplayer menus, and a built-in messenger." b="Squads and detailed player profiles." %} {% comparison_row label="Ads" a="One slot during play, none in the menus." b="Two slots during play, plus video ads. A short tutorial with the mascot, Guessy, covers how to play. Nothing is locked and no account is required. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic starts from a home screen that lays its modes out as tiles: highscore hunts, challenges, custom local and online games, the current season, and the Twitch mode. The two custom game tiles are locked until you reach supporter level 1 or until that month's API costs have been covered by other players. Most of the game expects an account. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot_pair image_1_url=menu_openguessr image_1_alt="The OpenGuessr menu overlay above a Street View panorama" image_1_caption="OpenGuessr's menu overlay, opened on top of a live round." image_2_url=menu_geotastic image_2_alt="The Geotastic home screen with its mode tiles" image_2_caption="Geotastic's home screen, with the two custom game tiles locked." %} {% #heading_primary %}Singleplayer and maps{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr's main singleplayer is endless: rounds follow each other with no menu in between, and a timer is optional, with a blink setting built into it, and a country streak runs alongside with an XP multiplier that grows as the streak does. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Its World map is rebuilt continuously with a generation server rather than by hand, so it always uses the latest Street View coverage, and the generator favours interesting and urban locations over long empty roads. The community library runs to tens of thousands of maps, organised into categories with search and sorting. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic sends you into a round through quick play or one of its structured formats, and back out to a results screen. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} One difference inside the round matters to serious players. Geotastic never shows road names, whereas OpenGuessr hides them only in NMPZ. Geotastic can do this because it uses the paid Google API. While road labels don't take away the round, the language used can be a good clue. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot image_url=maps_openguessr image_alt="The OpenGuessr map browser with base maps, special modes and a country list" caption="The OpenGuessr map browser. Scrolling further down shows the community maps." %} {% #heading_primary %}Modes and editors{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr goes wide by adding separate games. Country Guessr asks for the country rather than a pin. Image Guessr uses photographs instead of panoramas. Meta Guessr serves locations that contain well-known meta clues and explains them once you have guessed, so you pick up metas while playing. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Alongside those sits OpenGuessr Education, a free companion site with guides, quizzes and articles covering most individual countries, and it is source-available. Geometas, the site you are reading this on, is a separate platform by OpenGuessr built around flashcard-style meta quizzes that learn what you already know and show you more of what you do not. Geotastic has no equivalent. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot_pair image_1_url=guides_education image_1_alt="The OpenGuessr Education guides page" image_1_caption="Guides on OpenGuessr Education, sorted by difficulty." image_2_url=quizzes_education image_2_alt="The OpenGuessr Education quiz browser" image_2_caption="Quizzes for countries and regions." %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic goes wide by bending the classic round. Highscore hunts run across a range of rule sets, including select-country rules, country streaks and multiple-try formats. Seasons run on their own calendar, GeoBingo is a recent addition worth trying, and the Twitch mode hands the guessing to a streamer's chat. Several of these are original to Geotastic, and geography enthusiasts who like to tinker will get a lot out of them. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Both games let you build your own content, and they approach it from opposite ends. Geotastic's map and challenge editors are the more sophisticated pair, particularly for assembling large maps by hand. OpenGuessr has a competition editor and community map creation, and leans on letting you upload locations as JSON, so the heavy lifting happens outside the game. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #heading_primary %}Multiplayer{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr rooms are free to host and need no account from anyone in them. They run on PlaySocket, an open-source library built for the game, which keeps a room responsive: actions register immediately, late joiners sync to the correct state mid-game, and dropped players reconnect without stalling everyone else. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Hosts can kick, mute, and lock or unlock the room, and a room can be made public so it appears on the join page. A room can run with restrictions such as NMPZ, grayscale, hue shift, fade or inverted, and the same ones are available for competitions. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic's blitz duels are the standout, and experienced players like them because there is no waiting on a slower opponent and no walking around: you read the metas and commit. Quick play pulls in up to two friends in a few clicks. Full custom lobbies, local and online, sit behind supporter level 1, a 3 EUR donation, or behind the community covering that month's costs. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot image_url=multiplayer_openguessr image_alt="The OpenGuessr multiplayer screen with custom rooms and matchmade queues" caption="OpenGuessr multiplayer: custom rooms on the left, matchmaking on the right." %} {% #heading_primary %}Competitive play{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr runs a daily competition alongside hundreds of community-made competitions, any of which anyone can create, using the same restrictions available in rooms. Leaderboards show your friends' scores separately from the global ones. Tournaments run on top, free to enter and paying out in-game prizes. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Duels are five rounds with a multiplier at the end, wins pay elo and tokens, and a duel history keeps the round-by-round scores, both players' guesses and the original panoramas for later analysis. You also choose which pool you queue into, either your own division for an even match or everyone for a faster one. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic runs seasons with their own ranked play and a browsable season history, plus daily challenges alongside a large library of player-made ones, filterable by nearly every rule the editor exposes. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot_pair image_1_url=competitions_openguessr image_1_alt="The OpenGuessr competitions screen with the daily and community competitions" image_1_caption="OpenGuessr competitions." image_2_url=challenges_geotastic image_2_alt="The Geotastic challenge browser with its filter options" image_2_caption="Geotastic's challenge browser." %} {% #heading_primary %}What it costs{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr gates no features, which is what the Open in the name refers to. Supporter tiers at 3.99, 6.99 and 19.99 USD are bought once rather than subscribed to, and add a pin, a highlighted name, an XP boost and a badge. The 6.99 tier and up removes the ad. Every mode, map, room and competition works without it. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic is donation funded and open about it. Your account page shows your own costs against your own donations, and a negative balance means you play with ads. A 3 EUR contribution clears ads for a month even while the balance stays negative. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Supporter levels start at 3 EUR for the custom lobbies, 7 EUR adds badges, player cards and level 2 highscores, 15 EUR adds the level 3 versions, and the ladder continues to level 6. Perks are permanent once unlocked. A small share of players funds the game for everyone, which the developer has been straightforward about. It does mean a session can stop at a video ad or a locked tile. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot_pair image_1_url=supporter_openguessr image_1_alt="The OpenGuessr supporter page with three one-off tiers" image_1_caption="OpenGuessr supporter tiers, bought once rather than subscribed to." image_2_url=pricing_geotastic image_2_alt="The Geotastic donation page with a cost balance and supporter levels" image_2_caption="Geotastic's donation page and supporter levels." %} {% #heading_primary %}Friends and messaging{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} Both games let you add friends, and Geotastic has squads on top of that. On OpenGuessr 430,000 registered players use the social features with their friends, built around a messenger with threads, replies and reactions. It shows you which friends are online, and you can invite them straight from any multiplayer menu and have the invite reach them as a notification. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} The messaging works outside multiplayer too. You can send requests to players who are not friends yet, and anything unread is waiting with a red dot the next time you open the game. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #heading_primary %}Profiles and customization{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr has over forty pins once progression unlocks are counted, each one its own illustration built around a specific object or idea, earned by playing or bought with duel tokens. Badges work like achievements, awarded for things you pull off and shown in multiplayer and on your profile, next to flags, a description and a supporter label. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic gives a profile plenty to fill: trophies, seasons, achievements, statistics, created maps, played challenges and your own challenges. The interface around it is desktop-first and has a steeper learning curve than its rivals, which suits players who want the depth. There is no in-game music. {% /paragraph_text %} {% figure_screenshot_pair image_1_url=customization_openguessr image_1_alt="The OpenGuessr pin picker with progression and purchased rows" image_1_caption="OpenGuessr pins." image_2_url=customization_geotastic image_2_alt="A Geotastic user page with trophies, seasons and statistics" image_2_caption="A Geotastic profile." %} {% #heading_primary %}Design and feel{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr follows strict design guidelines, with tailormade components, hand-drawn illustrations, subtle interaction sounds and music composed for the game. All scrollable content fades out smoothly, powered by Overfade, and the panorama always shines through the UI so the focus stays on the round. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic builds on Material UI. The components are functional and familiar rather than distinctive, which works well for a game with this much packed into it. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #heading_primary %}So, which one is better?{% /heading_primary %} {% #paragraph_text %} OpenGuessr is the better pick if you want to start playing immediately and keep playing: endless singleplayer, a World map that stays current, several separate modes plus the Education quizzes and country articles, free rooms, competitions every day, no account and one ad slot. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Geotastic is the better pick if the rule sets and the editors are the appeal. Country streaks, highscore hunts, GeoBingo and blitz duels are formats you will not find elsewhere, the challenge editor behind them is the most detailed in the genre, and hidden road names make it the stricter training ground. Budget for a donation, or expect locked tiles and video ads. {% /paragraph_text %} {% #paragraph_text %} Our view is that OpenGuessr is the better free game and Geotastic the better sandbox, and that the sandbox is worth a donation if its formats are what you came for.
